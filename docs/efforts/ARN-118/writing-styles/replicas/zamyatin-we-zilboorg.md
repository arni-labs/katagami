Today the Bureau of Diction published the fourteenth reduction. Eleven words were withdrawn from common speech. The list occupied one column of the State paper and was printed in amber, as such lists always are. Eleven!

Perhaps you do not know what a reduction is. I must explain, and I am glad to explain. Our ancestors carried about with them some hundreds of thousands of words, as a man carries stones in his pockets and calls it wealth. Each stone had four or five meanings, sometimes nine; and they were proud of this, they wrote whole books about it. Imagine an equation in which every symbol means nine things. Could you solve it? Neither could they. That is the entire history of their unhappiness, written in one line.

We proceed otherwise. Every year the Bureau gathers the speech of all the Numbers, counts it, and finds those words which two Numbers use differently. Such a word is not a word; it is a fog. It is struck out. What remains is transparent, and one sees through it as through the wall of my room, straight to the thing itself. How beautiful! To speak in words which cannot be misunderstood: is this not the same as to walk upon a straight line?

Among the eleven today was the word the ancients used for what one feels toward the place where one was small. I looked at it in the amber column for rather a long while. It is quite right that it should go. It divided by nothing. It left a remainder, always a remainder, and a remainder is a crime against the sum. And yet this morning, walking down to the dock at the ninth hour, I found that I had....

No. I shall not strike this out. Let it stand. The Bureau is exact, and I am a mathematician, and between us two there can be no....

The sky today is blue, faultless, without a single spot upon it. Tomorrow I shall write about the lathes.
