On Saturday, March 14, 2020, about 10:47 a.m. mountain daylight time, carrier 2 of the Sable Ridge aerial passenger tramway separated from its haul rope near Harlow Pass, Colorado, and fell about 84 feet to the talus slope below. Of the 14 occupants, 3 were fatally injured and 11 sustained serious injuries; the carrier was destroyed. The tramway was owned by the Harlow Pass Recreation District and operated under contract by Vantic Mountain Transport LLC. The operator's summit anemometer recorded a temperature of 21°F and a wind from the west at 9 knots at 1045.

The tramway was placed in service in 1998 and carried two carriers on a single 1.34-inch-diameter track rope, each suspended from a four-wheel grip assembly. Operating records showed 612 hours since the most recent nondestructive examination of the grip assemblies, performed on October 3, 2019.

The drive terminal's data logger recorded line speed, motor current, and grip position at 1-second intervals. According to the logger, carrier 2 departed the summit terminal at 1043:22 at a line speed of 10.5 mph. At 1046:51, the recorded motor current rose from 148 amperes to 402 amperes over 4 seconds. Line speed fell to zero at 1047:03. The logger recorded no fault code before the current rise.

Two lift attendants and nine witnesses were interviewed. Attendants at both terminals stated that they heard a sharp report from tower 4 and then saw the carrier swing. One witness stated that the carrier "dropped straight, without tipping." Their accounts of the interval between the sound and the fall ranged from 2 to 8 seconds. The investigation did not establish that interval.

Examination of the wreckage found the forward grip jaw fractured through the clamping bolt hole. The fracture surface exhibited beach marks over about 62 percent of its area, consistent with fatigue; the remainder was consistent with overstress. The rear jaw was intact. No preimpact damage was found on the track rope, the haul rope, or the tower 4 sheave train. The tramway was not equipped, and was not required to be equipped, with a grip-force monitor.

Probable Cause The National Transportation Safety Board determines that the probable cause of the accident was the failure of Vantic Mountain Transport LLC to detect a fatigue crack in the forward grip jaw of carrier 2 during scheduled nondestructive examination, which resulted in the separation of the carrier from the haul rope. Contributing to the accident was the absence of a state requirement for grip-force monitoring on aerial passenger tramways.
