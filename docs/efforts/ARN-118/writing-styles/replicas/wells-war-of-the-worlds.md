The breaking of the Cawdon embankment was first noticed at about half past eight in the evening, by a signalman named Ferris, who was standing at his box above the Ings and had a clear view of the reservoir wall a mile and a quarter to the north. He said afterwards that the water came over the lip in one sheet, and that the noise of it reached him some seconds after he had seen the thing begin. I did not see this. I was at my desk in the counting-house at Nether Cawdon, three quarters of a mile below the wall, with the window open upon the mill yard, and the first I knew of the matter was a change in the light.

I went out into the yard. The men had already stopped their work and were looking north, up the line of the beck, where a brown wall perhaps twelve feet high was coming down the valley at the pace of a trotting horse. It carried the whole of Slaithe Bridge upon its front, turned edgeways, with the parapet still standing on it. I remember thinking that I must shut the ledger; and I went back and shut it, which cannot have taken more than four seconds, and afterwards I could never account for having done it at all.

Six of us got up on the drying-shed roof by the outside stair. From there I could see over the low roofs of Bank Street as far as the church, and I watched the water take the row of cottages at the foot of it, not by knocking them down, but by lifting them a little and then letting them fold. There was very little noise from the town itself. What I heard chiefly was a grinding, and under it a long steady rush, like a train that does not pass.

By nine o'clock the flood had gone by, and the valley below us was a lake with things standing in it. Turner, the foreman, said that he had counted eleven bodies going down; but he was greatly excited, and I myself saw four. The gasworks chimney stood until about half past ten and then went over towards the east, quite slowly. I came down at eleven, when the water was at my knees in the yard, and found the office clock still going, and my hat upon its peg, and the ink not spilled.
