We decided, in the end, to keep bees.

It was Pyke who proposed it, over supper, in the tone of a man mentioning a thing he has looked into. He said that a hive cost very little, that the bees did the whole of the work themselves, and that a man with two hives in a suburban garden might reasonably count upon eighty pounds of honey in a season. Marchmont asked whether they stung. Pyke said that they only stung when they were frightened, and that they were never frightened if you moved slowly and did not breathe upon them; and he went on to describe, with a good deal of feeling, the calm that settles over a man who has learnt to handle bees, until we were all quite ready to have some.

I ought to mention that Pyke had never kept a bee in his life. He had read a shilling manual on the subject at his aunt's, in 1884, while waiting for a train.

My own experience of livestock is confined to a tortoise, which I bought at Leadenhall Market when I was nineteen, and which I named Gladstone, for reasons that seemed sound at the time and that I have since forgotten. Gladstone was a disappointment. He did nothing whatever from March to October but sit in the shade of the water-butt with an expression of settled disapproval, and in October he went under the shed and stayed there till the following June, when he came out, looked at the garden, and went back. I fed him lettuce. He ate it in the manner of a man doing a favour for somebody he does not much care for. I once carried him three streets to show him to a friend, and he drew in his head at the corner of Warwick Road and would not come out again, so that I stood in a stranger's doorway for twenty minutes holding what any passer-by would have taken for a paving-stone. My friend was out. I carried him home again, and we did not speak on the way. That is the whole of my dealings with the animal kingdom before the bees, and I set it down here merely to show that I went into the business with an open mind.

Pyke ordered the hive on the Tuesday. It came on the Thursday, in eleven pieces, with a sheet of directions printed in what I take to have been Dutch.
