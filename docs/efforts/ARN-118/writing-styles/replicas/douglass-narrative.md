I was born in the village of Kottapalli, in the Godavari district, and I do not know the year. My mother reckoned it by the flood that took the lower fields, and that flood is written in no book that I have been able to find. At the depot in Madras the clerk set my age down as nineteen and asked nothing further. I have carried nineteen ever since, it being the only number concerning myself that exists upon paper.

The agreement was for five years. It gave a man a shilling for the day's task, and the task was to be such as an able man could finish in seven hours. Who was to say what an able man could finish, the paper did not state. This omission was not an accident; every hardship that came to us afterward came in through it.

We were eighty-four days at sea, and of the four hundred and ten who went aboard, nineteen were put over the side. I did not count them myself. I have the number from Ramsamy, who was in my mess and who counted everything, and I set it down as his and not as mine.

We were landed at Georgetown and sent up to Belle Vue estate on the Demerara coast. The manager there was Mr. Callender. The overseer under him was Mr. Prosser, and it is of Mr. Prosser that I have most to say.

In my second year a man of our gang, named Bhola, was taken with a fever and could not rise. Mr. Prosser came to the range at five and stood in the door and told him he might go to the hospital, and that the days would be entered against him as days not worked, and the shillings taken from his indenture at the end. Bhola got up and went to the field. He cut cane that day and the next, and on the third day he was carried in, and he died on the fourth. Mr. Prosser had struck no one and had broken no law of the colony. He had only said what the law of the colony permitted him to say.

That is the whole of the system. A man who is fined for being sick will work while he is sick; and a man who works while he is sick will be worked until he cannot, and the estate will have lost nothing by it, because there was another ship coming in the spring.
