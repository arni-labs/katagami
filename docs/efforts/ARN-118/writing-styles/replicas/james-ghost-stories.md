It was in the autumn of 1897, or possibly in the spring following—my note of the date is not what it ought to be—that Mr Aldersey came down into Herefordshire with the intention of making a list of the bells of the county. He was at that time a young man of thirty, with a small private income and an interest in founders' marks which his friends considered excessive, and he had the habit, common enough among people of his sort, of setting everything down in a quarto book which he carried in a satchel. The parish of Stallow Combe is not one that many people visit, and you will not find it in the guide-books. The church stands at the head of a lane, some three quarters of a mile from the village, and it has a squat western tower of the fifteenth century and a ring of five bells, of which the tenor is dated 1623. It was the tenor that Aldersey had come to see, for it bears upon its waist a mark which has been claimed, though upon no very good evidence, for one of the Gloucester foundries.

The rector, whose name I have been asked not to give, was away that week at his brother's, and the key had been left with the clerk, an elderly man called Rudge. Rudge was perfectly civil, and gave up his afternoon without complaint; but he would not go above the ringing chamber, and when Aldersey asked him the reason he said that the ladder was not what it had been. This was true. It was also true, as Aldersey discovered for himself within the hour, that the ladder had been renewed as far as the second stage in the course of the same year.

In the ringing chamber, hanging upon a nail beside the peal-boards, was a small ledger bound in vellum. It contained the accounts of the bell-frame, entered by a churchwarden of 1704 in a very tolerable hand, and at the foot of the third page there is an item which I give exactly as it stands: "Payd to Wm. Sayers for taking downe the sixth bell and for the burying of the same, 8s."

There is no sixth bell at Stallow Combe, and, so far as the registers go, there never has been one.
