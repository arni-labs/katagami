JULY 2.—Called at Fenner's, the ironmonger in the Grove, and ordered a garden roller, for I have made up my mind that the lawn at the back is a disgrace to the road. Emily thinks a roller an extravagance at eighteen shillings, but I pointed out to her that a good roller will last a lifetime, and is in the nature of an investment. By-the-by, that reminds me the side gate is off its hinge, and it must be seen to. Our neighbour, Mr. Sprigge, who is in the tea trade, looked over the palings while I was measuring the grass, and said nothing at all, though I am sure I gave him every opportunity.

JULY 3.—The roller came while I was at the office, and they had left it in the front garden by the steps, where it has made a rut which will have to be filled in. Emily said the boy would not take it round to the side, as he had nobody with him. I said that a man who cannot get a roller through a side gate was not a man I cared to employ, and that I should mention it to Fenner. I tried to move it myself before supper, and could not; and Sprigge, who was watering, remarked that I ought to have had the smaller size. I replied: "Perhaps; but I would rather roll than tickle." He went indoors without a word. I thought this rather good, and repeated it to Emily, who was cutting the bread and butter, and she did not look up.

JULY 5.—A most annoying thing. The roller has been over the border and taken the heads off eleven of my carnations, which were the pick of the bed. Emily says I should never have started it at the top of the slope. I say the slope is the fault of the builder, and I shall write to Mr. Dobney about it. Our nephew Percy called in the evening, wearing a straw hat with a coloured ribbon which I did not care for, and asked whether we had gone in for steam rolling. Emily laughed a good deal at this. I said nothing, but I looked at her, which meant more.

JULY 6.—Wrote to Mr. Dobney, and read the letter over twice before I sent it, as it was rather strongly worded. The lawn, I must say, is coming up beautifully in the flat parts. Sprigge has borrowed the roller.
