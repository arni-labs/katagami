I have known men who can tell the state of the tide by the smell of the mud, and I have envied them the trick, being one of those who must look it up in a book. At half-past four on the morning of the eleventh of April we lay in Watchet roads with the ebb making fast beneath us, and I was on deck in a borrowed oilskin, trying to look like a person who had been up since three. Pryce had in fact been up since three. He was coiling the peak halyard on its pin with the deliberation of a man who has done the same thing ten thousand times, and he did not look at me while he spoke.

"Neaps," he said. "We shan't get over the Gore before eight. You may as well sleep."

I did not sleep. I had spent six years in an underwriter's office in Water Street, pricing the sea at so many shillings in the hundred pounds, and I had built out of the tonnage returns a comfortable notion that I knew what I was insuring. The Bristol Channel took about four hours to correct me. It rises forty feet at springs off Portishead, and it does not rise politely; the flood comes up the narrowing funnel from Lundy like a wall on the move, and every bank in it—the Gore, the Culver, the long tail of the Nash—shifts a little each winter, so that a chart of 1897 is a work of history rather than of navigation.

At seven Pryce gave me the lead and set me to swing it from the weather shrouds. I made a poor business of it, and got the line about my boot, and was told about it. Then I began to enjoy myself. Three fathoms, and a quarter less three, and three again; and each time I brought the tallow up he glanced at what stuck to it and said nothing.

At the fourth cast it came up grey. Not sand at all, but a stiff blue clay, cold as putty, with a shell pressed into it.

Pryce took the lead out of my hand and looked a long while at the Somerset shore, two miles off and quite empty. Then he went below and got out the chart, and knelt on the locker with it, and put his thumb on the soundings there.

"The survey had this bank at eleven feet," he said. "Who has been down here since?"
