The Inspector of Bridges

An Inspector of Bridges, having examined a structure over which the traffic of a great city passed, reported it unsafe.

"You will be pleased to reconsider," said the Chairman of the Company. "The bridge has stood forty years."

"So it has," said the Inspector; "and I have stood but nine, and am already thought unreliable."

The Reputation and the Rumour

A Reputation, walking abroad in the evening, met a Rumour coming the other way.

"Stand aside," said the Reputation, "for I am established, and you are nobody's child."

"Sir," replied the Rumour, "I am on my way to your house, where I am expected to sleep. Your people have made up the bed."

The Philanthropist and the Assessor

A Philanthropist who had given a hospital to his town was waited upon by an Assessor of Taxes.

"The building is exempt," said the Philanthropist, "being devoted to the public good."

"That is why I have come," the Assessor answered. "Nothing else in this town is."

The Two Candidates

Two Candidates for the same office met upon a platform, and each in his turn assured the people that his opponent was a scoundrel unfit to be trusted with power.

"We have wronged each other," said the first, when the meeting was over. "Let us be reconciled, and say no more about it."

"Willingly," said the second; "for if the people should believe us both, the office would go to a stranger."

The Oyster and the Knife

An Oyster who had made a Pearl was visited by a Fisherman's Knife.

"You have laboured seven years," said the Knife, "and I am come to crown your industry."

"Do so," said the Oyster; "but I observe that in all your ceremonies the crown is taken off with the head."

The General and the Historian

A General who had lost a battle sent for a Historian.

"I wish the truth told," he said, "and nothing besides."

"Then I am the wrong man," said the Historian, "for the truth has been told already, and there is no money in a second edition."
