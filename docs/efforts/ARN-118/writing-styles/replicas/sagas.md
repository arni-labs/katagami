There was a man hight Eyvind, the son of Thorstein Redbeard, who dwelt at Otterholt east of the water; he was betrothed to Astrid, the daughter of Vigfus the Priest, and the wedding was set for the harvest.

Now there was another man named Thorbiorn Ravenswart. He was rich and hard, and had been out in viking three summers, and he came to Vigfus in the summertide with fifteen men and asked for the maiden; and Vigfus said the woman was promised already, and that a word given before witnesses is not undone for any man's asking, whereon Thorbiorn rode away and said nothing.

But when the gathering of the sheep was toward, and Vigfus was up in the fells with the more part of his household, Thorbiorn came to the garth at dawn with those same men, and took Astrid, and set her on a horse, and fared away west over the neck of the hill; and no man was slain there, but a thrall got a hurt from the flat of an axe, and it was he who bore the tidings into the fells.

Vigfus heard him out and asked how many had ridden.

"Fifteen," said the thrall, "and he that led them had a blue cloak."

"I know the cloak well," said Vigfus, "and the man under it. Ride thou to Otterholt and tell Eyvind, and bid him take counsel of no one older than himself."

So the word came to Eyvind at evening, and he gathered thirty men that night, and they rode west; and at daybreak they came down on Ravensdale, and there was Thorbiorn out on the ice of the water with eight men, driving horses over to the further shore. Then Eyvind rode onto the ice. Thorbiorn saw them and would not run, but set his men back to back, and the fight was on the ice, and three of Eyvind's company were slain, and of Thorbiorn's men six, and Thorbiorn last of all, and Eyvind took his sword and left the body where it lay.

Then they went up to the house and found Astrid, and she rode home with them.

At the Thing that summer Thorbiorn's brother sued for the slaying, and the case was given to arbiters, and they awarded that the taking of the woman went against the killing, and that Eyvind should pay for the two men slain beside Thorbiorn, and no more. Vigfus said that was a fair award.
