Then a well-digger said, Speak to us of Patience.

And he answered, saying:

You go down into your hill with a basket, and your basket comes up dry, and you say that the water has forgotten this place.

But the water has not forgotten you. It is your arm that is shorter than the night.

And what is patience but the stillness of the seed under your frost, that does all of its work in the dark and shows you none of it?

It is not the stillness of the stone, which is still because it has no feet.

The vine that you set in the spring shall give you no grape in that spring,

And the wood that you plane for a door shall not be a door until your house is standing about it,

And the baker does not open his oven to ask of the loaf whether it rises.

You would have the sheaf in the same week as the furrow, and the milk in the same hour as the calf.

But the seasons shall not shorten themselves for the man who is in haste, nor shall the river run twice through the wheel of his mill.

And when you are kept waiting at the gate of another, you say that the hour was taken out of your life.

That hour was your life, standing beside you in the road with the bridle of your horse in its hand.

What of the debt that is not yet paid, and the letter that is not yet answered, and the child that is not yet grown?

Call not your waiting an empty room.

It is a loom whose warp is already strung, and you are he who has not yet lifted the shuttle.

Often have I heard you say that the patient man is he who has learned to bear what he cannot lift.

But I say to you that there is a patience which is not the bearing of a weight, and it is theirs who love the thing that they are making.

The potter does not endure his wheel.

And the shepherd upon the hill in the third hour of the night does not endure his lamb.

Dig, then, and let the dry sand come up in your hands, and count it as water which has not yet come.

For the well knows not which stroke of your spade let the water in, and neither shall you.
