I set down here, with an unwillingness which I do not expect the reader to share, the substance of the papers left to me by my former tutor, Edmund Halloway, who died at Ithaca in the spring of 1931. The bequest reached me in a deal box with the seals unbroken, and I might have let it lie unopened for years had not the executor written that a portion of the contents was addressed to me by name. It was not a large collection: a bundle of field notes in pencil, four letters from a mining engineer at Perm, a cutting from a Boston paper for November 9th, 1929, and eleven wax cylinders wrapped separately in oiled paper and numbered in Halloway's hand from one to twelve, the seventh being absent.

The notes concern a boring made in the eastern Urals in the summer of 1929 under the direction of a company whose name I have thought it better to suppress. At a depth which Halloway gives, with a caution that now seems to me significant, as "not less than nine hundred feet, though the tally was disputed," the drill passed out of shale into a cavity; and the cavity was found to be floored with a black vitreous substance which no assay could place. Of this floor he says nothing further; and it is characteristic of him, and perhaps of the whole business, that the omission is nowhere explained.

What the cylinders hold I would rather not describe, and I am aware that such reluctance will be read as evasion. They were recorded, according to the engineer's letters, from an old man of a Vogul settlement on the Northern Sosva, who was said by his own people to be the last who could pronounce the invocations correctly, and who died within a fortnight of the recording. I have played four of them. The sounds are certainly speech, or the imitation of speech; they are not, so far as three philologists could determine, of any Uralic tongue, nor indeed of any tongue whose remotest affiliations they were prepared to guess at. It is the eighth cylinder which I have not been able to hear through, and which I have accordingly not destroyed, though I mean to.
