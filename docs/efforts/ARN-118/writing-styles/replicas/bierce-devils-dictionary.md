BARNACLE, n. A creature which, having found something that moves, resolves to be carried. Also, a nephew.

BREAKFAST, n. The meal at which a family discovers what it thinks of itself.

CHIMNEY, n. A monument raised by the householder to the memory of the forest that warmed him.

COMMITTEE, n. A body appointed to postpone with dignity what one man could have refused in a minute.

CONVALESCENCE, n. That interval of a malady in which the patient is well enough to receive the physician's bill and not yet strong enough to dispute it.

MAP, n. A picture of the earth executed by the nation that has taken the most of it, and coloured accordingly.

MOUSTACHE, n. A hedge behind which a weak chin conducts an orderly retreat.

He wore it broad, he wore it long,
He combed it morning, noon and night;
And when at last he did a wrong
The jury held his face was right.

Orm Pludder

PEDIGREE, n. A rope by which a gentleman is lowered into the past to bring up something that will hang him.

PILGRIM, n. One who leaves home in order to have somewhere to return from.

SIGNATURE, n. That portion of a contract which the signer has read.

TELESCOPE, n. An instrument for bringing a distant object near enough to disappoint. Astronomers, who own the largest of them, are the only persons able to prove that there is nothing in particular a long way off.

THIRST, n. The sensation that converts a moralist into a customer.

UMBRELLA, n. A portable roof, lent to a friend and thereafter the property of his heirs.

WEATHERCOCK, n. A public officer in iron, distinguished from those in flesh by turning only when the wind does.

WILL, n. A document by which a man endeavours to remain, after his death, the most interesting person in the family. It is the one composition of his life to which his relations give a close and reverent attention, and the only one whose errors are corrected by a court. When the late Ephraim Bandle of Cortland had lain a week in the ground, his three sons met in the office of his attorney to hear the instrument read, and learned that the whole estate had been bequeathed to a cat. They contested it, alleging that the animal was not of sound mind; the cat was represented by counsel, gave no evidence, and won.
