FISH MARKET

Mackerel on the crushed ice,
green and blue barred,
their eyes flat as coins.

The fishmonger's hands are red;
he wipes them on the sacking
and the sacking keeps the shape of his hands.

A gull walks the gutter
between the stalls,
turning its head sideways at the mackerel.

HAYFIELD, NOON

The mower has stopped.
His shirt hangs on the hedge,
white against the elder.

Grass falls in rows, still green underneath;
the crickets go out ahead of the blade
and come back behind it.

Heat over the stubble
shakes the far gate.

THE LADDER IN THE ORCHARD

Three rungs into the leaves
and the ground is gone.
Apples touch my wrist, cold on the shaded side.

Below, the basket;
above, more apples,
and a wasp going in and out of one that has split.

I come down with eleven.
The ladder stays where it is
till the light is off the wall.

WASHING LINE

Sheets, four of them,
blown out and slack, blown out again.
The pegs hold; the line dips at the middle.

A shirt fills with wind at the shoulders
and empties.
The grass beneath is dark and wet.

QUARRY

The face of the rock is white where they have cut,
grey where the rain has been on it a year.

A crane arm stands over the pit,
still, with a chain hanging.

Water in the bottom is green,
and the ledges go down into it in steps
that no one uses.

At noon the whistle;
the men come up the road in single file,
carrying tins.

CROSSING, DECEMBER

Ice on the puddles takes the print of a boot
and keeps it.

Two horses at the gate,
breath in front of their faces,
the near one shifting its weight from foot to foot.

THE STOVE

Coals settle.
Red goes down through the grate,
grey comes up over it.

The kettle stops singing
before the water is cold.
